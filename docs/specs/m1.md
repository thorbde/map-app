## Goal

The first milestone that produces something you can use: the world map as the base of the app, every country clickable, and a panel with each country's facts. No accounts and no essays yet (those are M2 to M4).

**Done when:**
- every country can be clicked in its continent view;
- the shell keeps the map mounted between pages;
- the size budgets hold: 150 KB of first-load JavaScript per page, and the lazily loaded map engine and the map file inside the limits set in PRs 3 and 4 (ADR 0020, ADR 0021).

## Where M1 stands (2026-11-17): read this before anything else

Written at the end of a very long session, for the session that takes over.

### What Thor has decided

- **MapLibre draws the map** (ADR 0021), with our own shapes and no tile service. It loads lazily, outside the 150 KB first-load budget, with a 500 KB limit of its own.
- **Equal Earth is the default projection for now,** registered as a custom projection. Mercator and the globe are later options. Equal Earth or Mercator as the final default is open.
- **One detailed map file, loaded once.** No levels of detail, no swapping while zooming: MapLibre simplifies for each zoom itself. A couple of seconds of loading is accepted. Phones are expected to use an app with the map files bundled.
- **Natural Earth's own files replace the `world-atlas` package.** "Kill world-atlas and lean in to Natural Earth."
- **Labels by rank, as on Google Earth, and no marker dots** (dots to be revisited).
- **The selected country is `color.teal.800`,** a deliberate exception to the 3:1 rule against written countries (`DESIGN.md`).
- **Contested territory never holds the work up:** prefer inclusion, record as high risk, don't ask (`CLAUDE.md`, ADR 0019).
- **Never reinvent what already exists:** every plan starts with "What already exists" (`CLAUDE.md`).
- **Thor has approved** how pan and zoom feel with the detailed file, on his laptop.

### Done: the map data is rebuilt on Natural Earth (thorbde/map-app#24)

- **`scripts/build-maps.ts` went from 605 lines to 380, about half of them comments and tables.** It downloads Natural Earth 5.1.2's files (pinned by checksum, cached in `apps/web/.cache/`), sorts the pieces into countries and neutral land, and hands the geometry to `mapshaper`. `world-atlas`, `topojson-client`, `topojson-simplify`, `d3-geo` and `iso-numeric.ts` are gone.
- **The file, `app/features/maps/geometry/world.json`** (1.98 MB gzipped; the type is in `map-file.ts`): 200 country features in order of ISO code, then one feature of neutral land. Each country has:

| Field | What | From |
|---|---|---|
| `iso` | Two-letter code, as in the country list | Natural Earth's `ISO_A2_EH` |
| `label` | `[lng, lat]` where the name goes. Seven are not on the country's own land, on purpose (ADR 0020) | `LABEL_X`, `LABEL_Y`. Kiribati's is hand-set on Tarawa |
| `labelMinZoom`, `labelMaxZoom` | The zooms the name shows between: Russia 1.7 to 5.2, Denmark 3 to 8, Luxembourg 5.7 to 10 | `MIN_LABEL`, `MAX_LABEL`, untouched |
| `labelRank` | 2 (most prominent) to 9 | `LABELRANK` |

- **`sizeKm` is gone,** with the marker dots.
- **Borders:** every place the tests pinned is where it was. What the newer data moved is in ADR 0020, "What moved when the source changed": Western Sahara's border by 600 m, and Bir Tawil and the Southern Patagonian Ice Field, which are now neutral.
- **The Cook Islands are at the far left of the map,** where they truly are. Niue is at the far right (longitude 190).
- **Three tests changed, each approved by Thor:** the label test (within 150 km of the country's land, the seven named), the `sizeKm` test (removed), and Morocco's southern edge (27.661, Natural Earth's line). The PR description has the details.

### Done: the map component is on the new data (thorbde/map-app#26)

- **Names by Natural Earth's zooms and ranks, placed by MapLibre.** `app/features/maps/labels.ts` hands MapLibre two numbers per country: the zoom its name shows from (`labelMinZoom` plus one overall offset) and its rank (`labelRank`). Where two names would overlap, MapLibre hides the lower-ranked one until there is room.
- **The offset is −1.5,** in `LABEL_ZOOM_OFFSET`. One level is arithmetic (Natural Earth counts the world as 256 px wide at zoom 0, MapLibre 512); the other half was set by eye from screenshots. **Thor corrects it on the preview:** lower shows more names, higher fewer.
- **A name stays once it has appeared.** `labelMaxZoom` is not used: other maps drop country names when zoomed in because city names take over, and we have none (Thor, 2026-11-17).
- **Written countries are named at every zoom** and go before every unwritten one. Two written names that would overlap still give way by rank. The selected country is always named.
- **No marker dots.** The dot images, their two layers, the click reach around them and everything that read `sizeKm` are gone. A country too small to click is opened by its name: the Vatican e2e test passes with its body unchanged (its title and comment now say "name"), and a new one opens a written Nauru by its name from the world view.
- **An unwritten microstate has neither a name nor a clickable shape until the viewer zooms to where its name appears,** as on Google Earth. Tonga, Vanuatu and the other small Pacific states are unnamed in the Oceania view for the same reason: Natural Earth ranks them with the microstates.
- **A country whose name is beside or between its land is framed by the land within 150 km of the name** (`countryBounds` in `views.ts`). Eight countries: New Zealand, Kiribati, the Vatican, Antigua and Barbuda, Equatorial Guinea, São Tomé and Príncipe, Trinidad and Tobago, St Vincent and the Grenadines.
- **The Oceania view runs from Australia to Niue and leaves the Cook Islands out** (Thor, 2026-11-17): they are at the map's left edge and are reached by panning. The test for continent views names them as the one exception and fails if a second appears. To be looked at again when the projection is settled.
- **`d3-geo` is back as a test-only package** (ADR 0013): the Equal Earth test checks our maths against it, and the data rebuild had removed it.
- **Sizes:** the map engine is 458.5 KB of its 500 KB limit, of which our own code is 3.1 KB.
- **Not asked for yet:** dashed disputed borders (Natural Earth's boundary-lines file) and the per-owner point of view. Both are listed in ADR 0020 as available.

### Things that cost time, so they don't again

- **A mouse moving over the map while it loads** once made the map give up. Fixed in #26, with an e2e test. Real visitors move the mouse; tests should too.
- **Vite must be told to bundle MapLibre's worker** (`?worker&url`, then `setWorkerUrl`), and must not inline small JSON files into the JavaScript.
- **A 6 MB JSON file can't be imported as a module in the test runtime** (workerd runs out of memory). Import it with `?raw` and parse it.
- **Node's `fetch` ignores the proxy here.** Run scripts that fetch with `NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY=1`. That includes the first `pnpm check` in a fresh session: it rebuilds the map, which downloads Natural Earth (30 MB) into `apps/web/.cache/`.
- **mapshaper has three traps,** all found the slow way:
  - Moving the map's edge with its documented commands (`-clip`, `-affine`, `-erase` with a box) silently drops the holes in countries, and then Lesotho and San Marino vanish.
  - `-rotate` does it properly, but only before `-simplify`. After it, fifteen countries vanish.
  - `-simplify interval=` only means metres while longitudes are within ±180.
- **`maps:check` compares the working tree with what is staged.** After `pnpm build:maps`, run `git add` on the map file before `pnpm check`.
- **e2e locally:** `PLAYWRIGHT_CHROMIUM_PATH=/opt/pw-browsers/chromium-1243/chrome-linux64/chrome pnpm test:e2e --trace off`. The design-system story tests can't start in a cloud session (the pinned browser build is missing); CI runs them.
- **`pnpm preview` on a port that is taken** fails quietly, and the old server then answers 500 for the rebuilt files. Stop the old one first (`pkill -f vite`).
- **The preview deploys can't be reached from a cloud session** unless `*.previews-d21.workers.dev` is allowed in the environment. Thor is the one who sees the real thing: ask him to look early.

## How to run M1: small sessions

The M0 session grew to about 800,000 tokens of context, and every step re-read all of it. A third of its tokens went on reading routine GitHub notifications. To avoid that:

- **One session per PR,** or per two or three small ones. Everything a session needs is in the repo.
- **Don't watch PRs for routine events.** Subscribe to a PR's activity only when it needs babysitting, and stop once CI is green and the review is handled. Thor says when something is merged or failing.
- **Keep bulky output out of the conversation:** search logs for the error instead of reading them whole, and save long output to a file.
- **Batch steps:** each tool round trip re-reads the whole conversation.

### A lead session, and one worker session per PR

Set up on 2026-11-17, so that Thor doesn't start sessions by hand.

- **Thor talks to one session, the lead.** It holds the decisions and the overview, and writes no code.
- **The lead starts a fresh worker session for each PR,** with a kickoff prompt and the branch to push to. The worker reads the repo, which is the only memory that carries over: `CLAUDE.md`, this file, the ADRs, the PR descriptions.
- **Plans go through the lead.** A worker writes its plan (starting with "What already exists") and sends it to the lead; the lead puts it in front of Thor in a few plain lines, and sends his answer back. **Since 2026-11-18 (Thor):** only strict-track plans wait for his answer; on the fast track the worker carries on.
- **Workers are short-lived (Thor, 2026-11-18):** one per PR, at medium effort, one message to the lead when done or blocked, archived at merge. Thor can also open a worker's session and answer there.
- **One worker at a time for stacked PRs.** Two sessions editing the same files collide.
- **The lead checks on its workers.** A session that finishes cleanly doesn't always report back.
- **The lead stays small.** It reads plans, summaries and PR descriptions, not diffs or logs. When it grows large all the same, it writes "Where M1 stands" afresh and Thor starts a new lead with the prompt below.
- **Every worker ends by updating "Where M1 stands"** in its own PR, so the next one starts from the truth.

**Prompt for a new lead:**

> You are the lead session for this repository. Read `CLAUDE.md` and `docs/specs/m1.md`, starting with "Where M1 stands" and "A lead session, and one worker session per PR". You write no code.
